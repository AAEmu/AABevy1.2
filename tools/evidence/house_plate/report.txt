     --multi-client --driver-dll <driver-dll> --hidden

3. Enter the world as <character>; place it next to the design-171 house at
   server (19650, 24385) for the original-client run. The client-side house is `Test Farmhouse`

Why
---
The client's `housings` table has 269 design ids; 231 of them are also
`npcs.id` values (tools/evidence/house_plate/collisions.csv). Design 171
("Test Farmhouse" in the English client) collides with NPC 171 ("Plains Wolf Pup"), whose
faction is 115 (hostile). The native client is the authority for what the
plate and unit data should be.

ID spaces (compact.sqlite3)
---------------------------
`tools/house_plate_collisions.py --db <game-db> ...` also writes the
`id_spaces` block in collisions.json:

* housings 269 rows, 231 ids also in `npcs`;
* slaves 140 rows, 127 ids also in `npcs`;
* transfers 117 rows, 106 ids also in `npcs`;
* shipyards 15 rows, 14 ids also in `npcs`.

Those numeric overlaps do not make the rows NPC templates: housing, slave,
transfer and shipyard are separate unit kinds and stay out of `npc_of`.
Mates are different: 156 `npcs` rows have `mate_kind_id <> 0`, including
the Brown Lilyut Horse (npc 5431, mate_kind 1, faction 2). The server's
`SummonMate { npc_id }` uses that id (server mates.rs:823). `item_summon_mates`
has 176 rows / 171 distinct npc ids; 21 rows point to missing npcs and 6
resolved rows have `mate_kind_id = 0` (stale data, not a different kind).
`npc_of` therefore accepts NPC and Mate, not Housing/Slave/Transfer/Shipyard.

Result (native client)
----------------------
house 111  Test Farmhouse   faction=friendly  type=housing
           kind=nil         level=1           category=farmhouse
           owner=<character>  hp=1500/1500  state=done
npc   ac2f Plains Wolf Pup  faction=hostile   type=npc  kind=beast
           level=2          hp=316/316       grade=normal

The house plate in the original-client crop (kept locally: real-client images are not committed) is yellow and reads "Test Farmhouse".
The NPC is hostile (red relationship). The house is therefore not treated
as the NPC row with the same numeric id by the native client.

Same-faction relation string
----------------------------
`system_faction_relations` has no (101, 101) row. `GameData::relation`
(data.rs:795) returns "friendly" for equal faction groups before consulting
the table; only then does `relation_of` convert friendly non-character units
of the player's own faction to "neutral" for the yellow plate.

The native house is faction 101 (the player's), and `GetUnitInfoById` says
`friendly`; the plate is yellow. A spawned same-faction NPC (10012,
`Lunar Halo Mate Fram`, client id 1f3b, BcId 7995) also reports
`faction=friendly` (type=npc, kind=human, level 19, hp 3255/3255) and also
has a yellow plate (local crop, not committed).

`plates::relation_of` maps every non-character unit that is friendly to the
player's own faction to "neutral" (the yellow-plate rule). That makes the Lua
`GetUnitInfoById(...).faction` string differ from the native client for every
same-faction NPC, not only for houses. This is broader pre-existing behaviour
and is deliberately not changed here; the plate colour is correct. Details:
same_faction.txt.

Bevy scenario (before and after the fix)
----------------------------------------
The server was restarted (runtime NPC 171 gone), character 1 was set to
server (19650, 24420, 168.9), roll pi, pitch/yaw 0, and two headless GPU runs
were made with no original client running.

Before (35e5ff82, parent of the fix):
  hud: plate 273 "Test Farmhouse": faction 115 (unit 101) own 101 -> hostile
  the plate crop is orange (0 yellow pixels of 27000)

After (07fc53c2, the fix; the house path is unchanged from the run):
  hud: plate 273 "Test Farmhouse": faction 101 (unit 101) own 101 -> neutral
  the plate crop is yellow (110 yellow pixels of 27000)

`house_plate_compare.py` prints PASS for all nine checks (native house
friendly/housing, native NPC hostile, before faction/relation, after
faction/relation, both crop colours). Raw runs and crops are kept under
data/extract/house_plate/house-plate-20261027-r1/bevy/ (git-ignored); the
evidence directory holds the plate crops and bevy_report.txt.
